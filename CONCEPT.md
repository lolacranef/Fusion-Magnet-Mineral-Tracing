# Magnet Mineral Rule Gaps

*Which laws reach the mining regions behind fusion superconducting magnets, and what each gap lets through, in plain terms. Rule terms checked 9 November 2026. Research, not legal advice.*

## Who this is for

### Primary: policy reviewers and public fusion funders
- **Who:** staff reviewing the EU Conflict Minerals Regulation and Japan's due diligence guidelines, officials building the EU forced labour risk database, and programme officers who fund fusion through DOE milestone awards, UK STEP and national research ministries.
- **Decision they make:** which minerals and regions a rule covers, and what conditions come with public money.
- **Why now:** the EU regulation is under review in 2026, Japan's review ends in 2026, and the EU forced labour ban starts in December 2027. Fusion buys tiny volumes and is publicly funded, so a sourcing condition costs little and reaches nearly every magnet maker.
- **Use it to:** show, region by region, where a mineral falls between laws, and in plain terms what that gap lets happen.

### Secondary: compliance and procurement at fusion companies
- **Decision:** what to ask their tape and wire suppliers, beyond what the law requires.
- **Use it to:** see which risks no law will flag for them, and build those questions into supplier contracts.

### Secondary: investigators, NGOs and journalists
- **Decision:** which tool to use against a documented abuse, such as a §307 petition to CBP or a sanctions referral.
- **Use it to:** match a region's evidence to the one rule that actually applies today.

## Verdict key

| Verdict | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Applies | The rule covers this region and mineral today |
| Partly reaches | Covers part of the problem, or only in some cases |
| Not yet in force | Would cover it, but not until a future date |
| Voluntary only | Recommends action but creates no legal duty |
| Doesn't reach | Out of scope by mineral, geography or type of harm |
| Not relevant | No connection to this region |

## The rules

### Dodd-Frank §1502 (US)

- **Minerals covered:** Tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold (from cassiterite, coltan, wolframite). Not niobium, not rare earths.
- **Where:** The DRC and its 9 neighbours: Angola, Burundi, CAR, Republic of Congo, Rwanda, South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia.
- **Who must act:** US-listed companies whose products need these minerals.
- **What it requires:** A good-faith inquiry into country of origin, disclosed on SEC Form SD. Disclosure only, no ban.
- **Status:** In force; 2025 reports were due 1 June 2026. The detailed Conflict Minerals Report has gone unenforced since a 2017 SEC staff statement, and the rule's future is uncertain.
- **Sources:** [Skadden (May 2026)](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/conflict-minerals-disclosures-due-june-1-2026) · [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/viewpoints/102kbie/is-u-s-conflict-minerals-disclosure-nearing-an-end) · [Claigan FAQ](https://www.claigan.com/conflict-minerals-faq/)

### EU Conflict Minerals Regulation (EU 2017/821)

- **Minerals covered:** Tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold: ores and metals only.
- **Where:** Conflict-affected and high-risk areas anywhere in the world. The EU's list of such areas is indicative, not binding.
- **Who must act:** EU importers of those ores and metals above volume thresholds. Not importers of finished parts like wire or magnets.
- **What it requires:** Supply chain due diligence based on the OECD Guidance.
- **Status:** Applies since January 2021.
- **Sources:** [Reg. 2017/821 Art. 1](https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2017/821/article/1/data.pdf) · [Ropes & Gray on CAHRA list](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2020/12/conflict-minerals-compliance-european-commission-publishes-global-list-of-conflict-affected) · [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/the-eu-new-mandatory-supply-chain-diligence-rules-batteries-raw-materials/)

### Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (EU 2026/470)

- **Minerals covered:** All. It covers human rights and environmental harm, not a mineral list.
- **Where:** The company's whole chain of activities, worldwide.
- **Who must act:** EU companies with 5,000+ staff and over €1.5bn turnover; non-EU companies with over €1.5bn turnover in the EU.
- **What it requires:** Scope where harm is most likely and severe, then assess in depth. Ending a relationship is no longer a required last resort.
- **Status:** Companies must comply by 26 July 2029. Fines capped at 3% of worldwide turnover; no EU-wide civil liability.
- **Sources:** [Covington (Feb 2026)](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/02/eu-csddd-csrd-omnibus-published-in-official-journal-transposition-delegated-acts-and-guidelines-are-next)

### EU Forced Labour Regulation (EU 2024/3015)

- **Minerals covered:** All products, every sector.
- **Where:** Any origin, any stage of the chain including extraction.
- **Who must act:** Anyone placing products on the EU market or exporting them.
- **What it requires:** Products made wholly or partly with forced labour are banned. Authorities carry the burden of proof, but refusing to cooperate counts against a company.
- **Status:** Applies from 14 December 2027. A public forced labour risk database is forthcoming.
- **Sources:** [Mayer Brown (Jun 2026)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/the-eu-forced-labour-regulation-10-questions-every-general-counsel-should-be-asking)

### EU Battery Regulation due diligence (EU 2023/1542)

- **Minerals covered:** Cobalt, natural graphite, lithium and nickel only.
- **Where:** Global.
- **Who must act:** Companies placing batteries on the EU market; a proposal would limit it to those above €150m turnover.
- **What it requires:** Raw-material supply chain due diligence.
- **Status:** Postponed to 18 August 2027; scope reduction still being negotiated.
- **Sources:** [A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/insights-publications/batteries-due-diligence-a-two-year-pause-and-reduced-scope) · [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/the-eu-new-mandatory-supply-chain-diligence-rules-batteries-raw-materials/)

### Tariff Act §307 import ban (US)

- **Minerals covered:** Any goods mined, produced or made by forced labour, including forced or indentured child labour.
- **Where:** Any country.
- **Who must act:** US importers. Anyone can petition CBP.
- **What it requires:** CBP can hold goods when information reasonably but not conclusively points to forced labour. The importer then has three months to show it made every reasonable effort to trace the labour.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Sources:** [CRS (Jul 2026)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-07-09_IF11360_c9e63009c902a08f9494a2def20f5e391c71b6fa.html)

### Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (US)

- **Minerals covered:** Any goods. Priority sectors include aluminium, copper, lithium and steel; rare earths are not named.
- **Where:** Xinjiang, plus companies on the UFLPA Entity List (187 after August 2026).
- **Who must act:** US importers.
- **What it requires:** Goods are presumed made with forced labour unless the importer proves otherwise with clear and convincing evidence.
- **Status:** In force; largest Entity List expansion took effect 3 August 2026.
- **Sources:** [Troutman (Aug 2026)](https://www.troutman.com/insights/dhs-adds-43-companies-to-uflpa-entity-list-in-largest-ever-single-expansion-effective-august-3/)

### Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG) (Germany)

- **Minerals covered:** All. It covers human rights and environmental risks.
- **Where:** Global, but risk analysis is normally limited to direct suppliers.
- **Who must act:** Companies in Germany with 1,000+ employees.
- **What it requires:** Risk analysis, prevention and remedy, and a complaints procedure.
- **Status:** Being cut back: reporting to the regulator abolished retroactively, only four kinds of breach stay fineable, and the regulator was told to pursue only serious cases. Amendment in the Bundestag since January 2026.
- **Sources:** [Taylor Wessing (Jan 2026)](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/01/lksg)

### Human Rights Due Diligence Guidelines (Japan)

- **Minerals covered:** All.
- **Where:** Global value chains.
- **Who must act:** Companies operating in Japan, which includes the largest HTS tape makers.
- **What it requires:** Due diligence in line with the UN Guiding Principles, as a recommendation.
- **Status:** Voluntary, no penalties. A government review is due to finish by end of 2026, with any bill in 2027.
- **Sources:** [HREDD tracker: Japan](https://hredd-org.pages.dev/tracker/japan-hrdd-guidelines)

### OECD Minerals Due Diligence Guidance (International)

- **Minerals covered:** All minerals.
- **Where:** All areas of the world.
- **Who must act:** Any company in a mineral supply chain.
- **What it requires:** A five-step due diligence framework.
- **Status:** Voluntary. It is the reference standard that binding laws like the EU Conflict Minerals Regulation build on.
- **Sources:** [International Tin Association on OECD](https://www.internationaltin.org/oecd/)

### Targeted sanctions (US / EU)

- **Minerals covered:** Not mineral-based. Targets named people, groups and companies.
- **Where:** Country programmes; see the region's entry.
- **Who must act:** Anyone dealing with a listed party.
- **What it requires:** No dealings with listed parties; assets frozen.
- **Status:** Varies by region.

### Rare earth export controls (China)

- **Minerals covered:** Seven medium and heavy rare earths, including yttrium and gadolinium.
- **Where:** Exports from China.
- **Who must act:** Chinese exporters, who need a licence for each shipment.
- **What it requires:** Export licence. The test is strategic, with no human rights condition.
- **Status:** Fully enforced since April 2025.
- **Sources:** [TariffCenter guide](https://tariffcenter.ai/blog/china-2026-rare-earth-export-controls-guide)

### Producer-country enforcement (Local)

- **Minerals covered:** Whatever is mined there.
- **Where:** Within the country.
- **Who must act:** Miners, traders and officials.
- **What it requires:** Mining and trading under national law.
- **Status:** Varies by region.

## Region: Kachin State, Myanmar

*Yttrium · heavy rare earths*

- **What is mined:** Heavy rare earths, including yttrium, mined from shallow clay deposits. Yttrium is the "Y" in the YBCO tape inside compact fusion magnets.
- **Who controls it:** Militias aligned with the junta brokered the trade; in 2024 the Kachin Independence Army seized sites producing about half the world's heavy rare earths.
- **Documented harm:**
  - More than 2,700 mining pools spread over an area the size of Singapore since 2016
  - Toxic waste reported flowing into the N'Mai Kha, a tributary of the Irrawaddy
  - Mining revenue at high risk of funding military abuses
  - Workers protesting wage cuts and irregular pay
- **Where it goes:** Trucked across the border to Yunnan and separated in Chinese facilities. China supplies 93% of US yttrium imports.
- **Fusion link:** Yttrium and gadolinium go into REBCO tape used by Commonwealth Fusion Systems, Proxima Fusion, Tokamak Energy and the UK STEP programme.
- **Sources:** [Mongabay on Global Witness](https://news.mongabay.com/2022/08/toxic-rare-earth-mines-fuel-deforestation-rights-abuses-in-myanmar-report-says/) · [NBC/CNBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/how-a-war-torn-myanmar-plays-a-critical-role-in-chinas-rare-earth-dominance/3854510/?os=ave) · [Khaosod on UN report](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/international/2026/08/28/un-myanmar-rights-crisis-hits-new-low-as-minorities-face-brutal-abuses/amp/) · [BNI](https://www.bnionline.net/en/node/113988) · [USGS yttrium](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-yttrium.pdf)

**Coverage tally:** 5 partly reaches, 1 not yet in force, 2 voluntary only, 5 doesn't reach

| Rule | Verdict | What it says here | What the gap lets through |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dodd-Frank §1502 | Doesn't reach | Rare earths aren't on the list, and Myanmar isn't a covered country. | A US company can buy yttrium-based tape without ever asking where the yttrium was dug up. |
| EU Conflict Minerals Regulation | Doesn't reach | It reaches conflict areas anywhere, so Kachin qualifies on its own principles. But only tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold count. | Myanmar heavy rare earths enter the EU with no due diligence duty, while tin from the same conflict would need it. |
| Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive | Not yet in force | It would cover pollution and conflict financing in the chain, but only from July 2029 and only for very large companies. | Fusion start-ups and tape makers are far below 5,000 staff and €1.5bn turnover, so the companies closest to the risk are out of scope. |
| EU Forced Labour Regulation | Partly reaches | From December 2027 it bans products with forced labour at any stage. | Most documented harm in Kachin is pollution, illegal mining and armed-group revenue. None of that is forced labour, so the ban can't touch it. |
| EU Battery Regulation due diligence | Doesn't reach | It covers only cobalt, graphite, lithium and nickel. | Rare earths in magnets and superconductors sit outside even the EU's most mineral-specific law. |
| Tariff Act §307 import ban | Partly reaches | It bans forced-labour goods from any country. | Same limit as the EU ban: environmental destruction and conflict financing are not forced labour. |
| Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act | Doesn't reach | The presumption applies only to Xinjiang and listed companies. | The US's strongest import tool flips the burden of proof only for Xinjiang. Myanmar ore gets no presumption. |
| Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG) | Partly reaches | German companies with 1,000+ staff must analyse risk, normally at direct suppliers. | A German buyer of HTS tape checks its tape maker, not the mine four tiers up. Its reporting duty is being abolished. |
| Human Rights Due Diligence Guidelines | Voluntary only | Japan asks companies to do due diligence but sets no legal duty. | Japan hosts the largest HTS tape makers. Nothing legally requires them to trace their yttrium. |
| OECD Minerals Due Diligence Guidance | Voluntary only | The Guidance asks for due diligence on all minerals in all areas. | It is the right standard, but nobody is obliged to apply it to rare earths. |
| Targeted sanctions | Partly reaches | EU: asset freezes, travel bans and an arms embargo on 105 people and 22 entities, extended to May 2027. The US removed four junta-linked parties from its list in July 2025. ([The Star (Apr 2026)](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/04/27/eu-extends-sanctions-against-myanmar-by-a-year), [ThePrint (Jul 2025)](https://theprint.in/world/us-lifts-some-myanmar-sanctions-after-general-asked-trump-for-relief/2702840)) | No measure targets the rare earth trade itself, so money from the mines can move without touching a listed name. |
| Rare earth export controls | Partly reaches | China licenses every export of yttrium and gadolinium. | The licence is about leverage, not abuses. Once Myanmar ore is separated in China it leaves as controlled Chinese material, and its origin disappears. |
| Producer-country enforcement | Doesn't reach | Global Witness found the mines illegal under Myanmar law. ([Mongabay on Global Witness](https://news.mongabay.com/2022/08/toxic-rare-earth-mines-fuel-deforestation-rights-abuses-in-myanmar-report-says/)) | After the 2021 coup the rule of law collapsed, so illegality on paper has no effect on the ground. |

## Region: Shan State & Wa, Myanmar

*Rare earths · tin*

- **What is mined:** Rare earths, and most of Myanmar's tin, which comes from Wa State. Tin goes into the niobium-tin wire of ITER-style magnets.
- **Who controls it:** The United Wa State Army controls large areas; mines are Chinese-owned or Chinese-run.
- **Documented harm:**
  - Trafficked scam-centre workers held with shackled convicts and forced to dig at rare earth mines (reported May 2026)
  - Rare earth sites in Shan rose from 12 before the 2021 coup to 85 after
  - Toxic runoff contaminating the Kok and Salween rivers
- **Where it goes:** Rare earths and tin concentrate move into China for separation and smelting. Wa State paused mining in August 2023; tin shipments were expected to resume in 2025.
- **Fusion link:** Rare earths feed REBCO tape; tin feeds Nb₃Sn wire like that in ITER's toroidal field coils.
- **Sources:** [BHRRC on SHRF](https://www.bhrrc.org/de/neuste-meldungen/myanmar-uwsa-alleged-of-trafficking-scam-workers-into-chinese-operated-rare-earth-mines/) · [Karen News](https://karennews.org/?p=12460) · [Khaosod on UN report](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/international/2026/08/28/un-myanmar-rights-crisis-hits-new-low-as-minorities-face-brutal-abuses/amp/) · [Irrawaddy](https://www.irrawaddy.com/?p=153906) · [Irrawaddy on Wa tin](https://www.irrawaddy.com/?p=127037) · [Mining Weekly (Jul 2025)](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/tin-shipments-from-myanmars-wa-state-due-to-resume-tin-group-says-2025-07-16)

**Coverage tally:** 1 applies, 3 partly reaches, 2 not yet in force, 2 voluntary only, 5 doesn't reach

| Rule | Verdict | What it says here | What the gap lets through |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dodd-Frank §1502 | Doesn't reach | Tin counts, but only from the DRC region. | Wa tin can end up in US products with no disclosure at all. |
| EU Conflict Minerals Regulation | Partly reaches | EU importers of tin ores and metals must do due diligence on conflict areas anywhere, which should include Wa State. | It stops at the importer of raw tin. Wire and magnets made from it aren't covered, and rare earths from forced-labour mines are outside entirely. |
| Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive | Not yet in force | Forced labour and pollution are in scope from July 2029 for very large companies. | Nothing happens before 2029, and most fusion suppliers are too small to be covered. |
| EU Forced Labour Regulation | Not yet in force | Documented forced labour at mines is exactly what this ban targets, from December 2027. | Until then the EU has no tool. Afterwards, authorities still have to prove the link product by product. |
| EU Battery Regulation due diligence | Doesn't reach | It covers only cobalt, graphite, lithium and nickel. | Neither rare earths nor tin are in scope. |
| Tariff Act §307 import ban | Applies | Forced labour at a mine anywhere is enough. Anyone can petition CBP, which can hold goods on reasonable but not conclusive evidence. | The law fits today, but someone has to tie the mine to specific imported goods, and separation in China makes that hard. |
| Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act | Doesn't reach | Xinjiang and listed companies only. | There is no presumption for Myanmar, even with forced labour documented. |
| Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG) | Partly reaches | Risk analysis normally stops at direct suppliers. | A German magnet buyer is not expected to look past its first-tier supplier toward Wa State. |
| Human Rights Due Diligence Guidelines | Voluntary only | Recommendation only. | Japanese tape and wire makers face no legal duty to check for forced labour upstream. |
| OECD Minerals Due Diligence Guidance | Voluntary only | Covers all minerals, all areas. | Correct standard, no obligation. |
| Targeted sanctions | Doesn't reach | None of the US or EU measures reviewed here target Wa State's mineral trade. ([The Star (Apr 2026)](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/world/2026/04/27/eu-extends-sanctions-against-myanmar-by-a-year)) | The armed group profiting from the mines faces no mineral-specific restriction in these programmes. |
| Rare earth export controls | Partly reaches | Rare earth exports from China need a licence; tin does not fall under these controls. | Separation in China turns Myanmar ore into licensed Chinese product, which erases where it was mined. |
| Producer-country enforcement | Doesn't reach | Wa State suspended all mining in August 2023 to formalise the sector. ([Irrawaddy on Wa tin](https://www.irrawaddy.com/?p=127037)) | The armed group is the regulator, so formalisation serves its revenue rather than workers' rights. |

## Region: Bangka Belitung, Indonesia

*Tin*

- **What is mined:** Tin from onshore pits and offshore dredging. Around 90% of Indonesia's tin comes from these islands.
- **Who controls it:** State tin company PT Timah holds permits; most small-scale mining is informal or illegal.
- **Documented harm:**
  - An estimated 6,300 child labourers in tin mining (ILO, 2015)
  - 57 deaths in abandoned tin pits, many of them children
  - About 240,000 hectares of mangrove damaged
  - A tin corruption case with state losses put at Rp300 trillion
- **Where it goes:** Smelted in Indonesia and exported as refined tin, a large share of world trade.
- **Fusion link:** Tin goes into Nb₃Sn superconducting wire used in ITER and other low-temperature magnets.
- **Sources:** [Tin mining in Indonesia](https://wiki.riteme.site/wiki/Tin_mining_in_Indonesia) · [Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/ms/news/grim-toll-from-indonesias-abandoned-mines-may-get-even-worse-report/) · [VOI (Jul 2025)](https://voi.id/en/amp/491796)

**Coverage tally:** 1 applies, 4 partly reaches, 1 not yet in force, 2 voluntary only, 3 doesn't reach, 2 not relevant

| Rule | Verdict | What it says here | What the gap lets through |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dodd-Frank §1502 | Doesn't reach | Tin counts, but Indonesia isn't a covered country. | US companies need not ask whether their tin came from Bangka. |
| EU Conflict Minerals Regulation | Partly reaches | Tin is covered, and high-risk areas can include widespread abuses. Whether Bangka counts depends on each importer's own risk assessment. | If an importer decides Bangka isn't high-risk, nothing more is required. |
| Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive | Not yet in force | Child labour and environmental damage are in scope from July 2029. | The companies buying magnet wire today face no duty for years, and most are too small to be covered. |
| EU Forced Labour Regulation | Partly reaches | From December 2027 it bans forced child labour. | Hazardous child labour and deadly pits are not automatically forced labour, so they can fall outside. |
| EU Battery Regulation due diligence | Doesn't reach | Tin is not a battery raw material under the law. | Not in scope. |
| Tariff Act §307 import ban | Partly reaches | It covers forced or indentured child labour. | Children working in dangerous pits for family income may not meet the forced labour test, so the ban may not apply. |
| Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act | Doesn't reach | Xinjiang only. | Not relevant to Indonesian tin. |
| Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG) | Partly reaches | Child labour is in scope, but risk analysis normally stops at direct suppliers. | A German wire buyer is not expected to trace its tin back to Bangka's pits. |
| Human Rights Due Diligence Guidelines | Voluntary only | Recommendation only. | No legal duty for Japanese wire makers to check tin origin. |
| OECD Minerals Due Diligence Guidance | Voluntary only | Covers tin and all areas; the tin industry built its programmes on it. | Voluntary outside the EU importer stage. |
| Targeted sanctions | Not relevant | No sanctions programme applies. | This is a domestic governance problem, not a sanctions one. |
| Rare earth export controls | Not relevant | Tin isn't among China's controlled rare earths. | Not relevant here. |
| Producer-country enforcement | Applies | Courts sentenced businessman Harvey Moeis to 20 years over tin trading in PT Timah's permit area from 2015 to 2022. In September 2025 the president ordered about 1,000 illegal mines shut and their smuggling routes cut. ([VOI (Jul 2025)](https://voi.id/en/amp/491796), [Antara (Sep 2025)](https://en.antaranews.com/news/383205/govt-calls-for-shutdown-of-1000-illegal-tin-mines-in-bangka-belitung)) | Illegal ore was sold under a state company's legal permit, so the legal paperwork was itself the laundering step. |

## Region: Rubaya, North Kivu, DR Congo → Rwanda

*Coltan (niobium, tantalum)*

- **What is mined:** Coltan, the ore of tantalum and niobium. Rubaya produces around 15% of the world's coltan.
- **Who controls it:** The Rwanda-backed M23 has controlled the mines since late April 2024. Before that, the PARECO-FF militia controlled them.
- **Documented harm:**
  - Summary killings, torture, arbitrary detention, forced labour and forced recruitment documented by Amnesty
  - An estimated $800,000 a month in levies financing M23
  - Child labour common, and frequent deadly accidents
- **Where it goes:** Smuggled into Rwanda, whose official coltan exports doubled after M23's takeover, then refined in the UAE or China and sold on as their metal.
- **Fusion link:** Niobium goes into Nb₃Sn and NbTi magnet wire. About 90% of niobium comes from Brazil, so Rubaya is a smaller but high-risk source.
- **Sources:** [Amnesty (Sep 2026)](https://www.amnestyusa.org/reports/drc-we-didnt-come-to-play-m23-abuses-at-artisanal-mines-and-the-trafficking-of-gold-and-coltan-to-rwanda/) · [Global Witness](https://globalwitness.org/en/press-releases/deadly-drc-mine-disaster-highlights-human-cost-of-coltan/) · [AP via Africanews](https://www.africanews.com/amp/2025/05/19/congos-coltan-miners-dig-for-worlds-tech-amid-struggles/) · [Reuters via Monitor](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/inside-the-mine-that-feeds-the-tech-world-and-funds-congo-s-rebels-5154474) · [SFA Oxford](https://www.sfa-oxford.com/market-news-and-insights/niobium-swing-producer-cbmm-driving-the-future-of-advanced-materials/)

**Coverage tally:** 2 applies, 3 partly reaches, 2 not yet in force, 2 voluntary only, 3 doesn't reach, 1 not relevant
